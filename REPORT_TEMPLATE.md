# This Week in Agents — YYYY-MM-DD

> **Coverage:** YYYY-MM-DD through YYYY-MM-DD · Preference: primary evidence, reproducible evaluations, and technically material developments.

## The 90-second brief

Use a compact table with 5–8 signals: **signal**, **what changed**, and **research relevance**. This is the reader's map, not a duplicate abstract.

## Weekly thesis

Write 1–2 paragraphs identifying the most important connection across the week's items. Separate observation from inference.

## Deep reads

Select roughly 6–10 papers, reports, standards, or evaluations that clear a high novelty and evidence threshold. For each:

**Authors/organization · publication date · source type**

- **Sources:** [primary](URL) · [code/data](URL)

## Research radar

Use for approximately 4–8 promising same-week items that are relevant but too preliminary, incremental, or lightly validated for the main shortlist. Give each a compact paragraph and direct primary link.

## Engineering and field notes

Cover approximately 4–8 substantive implementations, postmortems, repositories, or practitioner analyses. Prefer concrete architecture, operational evidence, or transferable patterns. Label vendor claims.

## Agent news and incidents

Cover approximately 8–12 material developments when the week supports them. For each state:

- **What happened — event date:** Verified fact.
- **Why it matters:** Technical or research consequence.
- **Evidence boundary:** What is vendor-supplied, preliminary, contested, or inferred.
- **Sources:** Direct primary source, plus independent reporting when valuable.

## Signals and implications

Give 3–5 cross-item deductions and label each **Synthesis** or **Inference**.

## What this changes for the research agenda

Translate the week's evidence into concrete evaluation questions, design experiments, datasets, or monitoring requirements.

## Watch next week

List only concrete forthcoming releases, deadlines, replications, rollouts, or unresolved claims.

## Coverage note

Disclose which standing-source families were checked, gaps or access limitations, and why the final count is above or below the normal range. Opening a landing page is not a completed source check; inspect dated items or feeds.

## Pre-publication checklist

- Run two passes: the standing source registry, then an independent broad discovery sweep.
- Check each Tier A source family; rotate Tier B sources and follow strong signals.
- Verify every date, title, author/organization, and direct link.
- Prefer primary sources; use analysis only when it adds independent value.
- Deduplicate announcements, papers, repositories, and coverage of the same event.
- Distinguish peer review, preprint, vendor report, journalism, and commentary.
- Label vendor-supplied, preliminary, contested, or unreplicated claims.
- Do not meet section ranges with weak material. Explain thin weeks in the coverage note.
- Confirm every cited link appears in the ledger and every ledger link is used.
- Update the README index and the existing edition file rather than creating duplicates.
